# Democratic Configurational Intelligence

This repository develops **Democratic Configurational Intelligence (DCI)**: a way of making configurational knowledge usable and revisable from the positions of people governed, burdened or otherwise affected by an arrangement.

DCI begins from a distinction between making knowledge available and changing the position from which that knowledge operates. Strategic or administrative knowledge may be widely accessible while still presuming the position of a ruler, commander, manager or central observer. Access alone does not change whose purposes organise the field, who appears as an acting subject, or whose knowledge can revise the arrangement.

The project asks how configurational perception can be democratically transposed without losing its capacity to read an arrangement as an operative whole.

## Central proposition

> Democracy gives configurational knowledge its political answerability to individuals; 势 gives democracy the capacity to perceive how an arrangement conditions whether individuals can appear, act and correct it.

## The problem

Democratic systems affirm the importance of individuals, but this commitment does not automatically provide a way to perceive how an arrangement forms, acquires direction and makes some actions easier, faster or more authoritative than others.

Configurational and strategic traditions can perceive these relations, but they have often been organised from the position of those who arrange or govern the field. When this knowledge is merely distributed, readers may be invited to imagine the ruler's position without receiving the ruler's actual authority, resources or field of action.

DCI therefore distinguishes:

- **distributive democratisation:** widening access to existing knowledge;
- **democratic transposition:** changing the political orientation and practical use of configurational knowledge;
- **positional democratisation:** testing whether affected positions can perceive, contest and revise the arrangement without adopting the ruler's seat; and
- **positional renewal:** ensuring that no account acquires permanent representative authority merely because its author later gains institutional recognition.

## What DCI contributes

DCI develops a capacity to examine:

- how connected conditions compose an operative configuration;
- how a configuration acquires direction and momentum;
- what the arrangement makes easier, harder, visible or dismissible;
- how individuals are enabled or prevented from appearing as consequential actors;
- how knowledge changes function when it moves between positions;
- how critique can be displaced from authority-producing arrangements onto less powerful readers or participants;
- how feedback is admitted, translated, protected or neutralised; and
- what would allow knowledge from non-controlling positions to change the configuration.

## Configurational concepts

The paper draws selectively on Chinese configurational thinking, including:

- **形** — what has already taken form or become sedimented;
- **位／连** — position and connection;
- **势／向** — accumulated direction, momentum and operative propensity;
- **机／权** — consequential openings, leverage and changing practical possibility; and
- **化／变** — transformation and evidenced change.

These concepts are used as contemporary analytical resources. Their cultural origin is acknowledged, but origin alone is not treated as evidence that an interpretation or application is valid.

## Working definition

> Democratic configurational intelligence is the distributed, positionally renewable capacity to perceive and revise how an operatively integrated arrangement composes itself and conditions the appearance, action and corrective standing of individuals, including through field responses that its authorised representation may exclude.

Feedback counts as corrective only when the conditions of its production, travel, protection and alteration power allow it to change the configuration.

## Relationship to Jurisdictional Recession

DCI provides part of the methodological ground for **Configurational Jurisdiction Inquiry**. It explains why an inquiry should not assume that one institutional or research position possesses the complete configuration, and why participation must be capable of revising the represented object rather than merely supplying information to an authorised observer.

The proposed PhD remains separately bounded around jurisdictional recession, the Robodebt worked case and the development of a configurational inquiry method.
