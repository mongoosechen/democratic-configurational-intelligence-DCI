# Democratic Configurational Intelligence

*Democratically transposing configurational knowledge, reconstructing the organism,  
and protecting the appearance of individuals*

**Mongoose Chen · Working concept paper · August 2026**

> **CENTRAL PROPOSITION**  
> Democracy gives configurational knowledge its political answerability to individuals; 势 gives democracy the capacity to perceive how an arrangement conditions whether individuals can appear, act and correct it.

**Conceptual status:** Developing framework

## 1. Purpose

This paper develops a concept of democratising knowledge that goes beyond widening access. Its starting point is that knowledge does not merely describe an object. Actionable knowledge also presumes a position from which the object can be seen, used and changed. Strategic knowledge such as The Art of War is publicly available, yet its operational seat remains largely that of the ruler or commander: the person who arranges circumstances and acts through others.

The proposal here is not to discard this knowledge because some of its expressions have been organised around command. It is to transpose its political orientation. The configurational perception associated with 势 can be made usable from the positions of people who are governed or burdened by an arrangement, enabling them to perceive, contest and alter how it acts through them. At the same time, this perception can address a weakness within democratic systems: their commitment to the importance of individuals does not automatically give them the capacity to perceive the system as an operatively integrated, living arrangement.

The resulting capacity is called democratic configurational intelligence. Democratic transposition names the constructive movement through which configurational knowledge is reoriented from control toward distributed perception and correction. Positional democratisation names its internal test: whether the knowledge’s implied seat, assumed capacities, visible effects and conditions of revisability have actually changed.

## 2. The problem with ordinary knowledge democratisation

Knowledge democratisation usually means making knowledge available to more people: removing barriers of cost, education, language, institutional membership or technical expertise. This is distributive democratisation. It is valuable, but incomplete. A body of knowledge may be distributed universally while continuing to present a privileged position as its implied operational seat.

A strategic manual made freely available to everyone may invite each reader to imagine being the commander. Those who are commanded become visible only as terrain, morale, labour, risk, resistance or capacity. Yet the invitation does not give every reader the commander's actual authority, resources or field of action. Access has changed; the internal political architecture of the knowledge has not, and the reader's material position may not have changed either.

**Distributive democratisation.** Widening access to an existing body of knowledge without necessarily changing the position, capacities or political purpose that the knowledge presumes.

**Democratic transposition.** Preserving a body of knowledge’s configurational capacity while changing its political orientation so that it becomes usable and revisable from positions governed or burdened by the arrangement.

**Positional democratisation.** The test of whether actionable knowledge can be perceived, contested and revised from materially affected positions without requiring those people to adopt the seat of ruler, commander, administrator or designer.

**Implied operational seat.** The position, capacities and field of control that a body of actionable knowledge silently assigns to its expected user.

> **KEY DISTINCTION**  
> Making the ruler’s knowledge available to everyone does not yet remove the ruler from the knowledge.

### 2.1 Positional non-equivalence in the circulation of knowledge

Knowledge does not carry its producer's full position with it. A reader may acquire an author's concepts without acquiring the recognition, institutional access, resources, protection or conversion capacity through which the author turned those concepts into authority. The text may offer an imagined operational seat, but reception does not by itself install the reader in that seat.

The operative function of knowledge can therefore change as it travels. An abstract commitment to humanity might help an elite author accumulate moral standing while enabling a socially isolated reader to sustain a relationship to the world that immediate personal relations cannot support. The words may be shared while their uses, dependencies, risks and consequences remain positionally different. Reading, admiration or fandom does not establish equivalence of motive or operation.

This also changes where criticism should attach. A criticism may correctly identify that an author used universal ideals to avoid responsibility toward particular people or to convert moral language into status. It becomes configurationally misplaced when it is transferred automatically to readers who depend upon the work but possess none of the author's authority-producing capacities. The fault identified upstream can then be imposed on accessible downstream recipients while the arrangement that rewarded the author remains intact.

Democratic configurational intelligence must therefore follow the career of knowledge across production, transmission and uptake. It asks what position enabled the account to be produced, how institutions amplified it, what it enables from a particular receiving position, whether the recipient can convert it into authority, and where praise, suspicion or fault is subsequently directed. The relevant unit is not the text or declared belief alone, but the changing relation between knowledge and position.

**Positional non-equivalence of uptake.** The principle that circulation of knowledge does not automatically distribute the position, capacities, motives or advantages of its recognised producer. The operative function of knowledge must be examined again at each position through which it travels.

**Critique displacement.** The movement of criticism from the position and operation that produced an identified problem toward more accessible downstream recipients who may not possess the relevant authority or benefit.

> **CRITIQUE-LOCATION PRINCIPLE**  
> Attach criticism to the position and operation through which knowledge produces the identified problem. Do not infer positional or moral equivalence from reading, admiration or shared language alone.

## 3. Two incomplete forms of political perception

Democratic thought and the strategic knowledge of 势 begin from different strengths. Democracy begins from the political significance of individuals. Strategic configurational knowledge begins from the way an arrangement operates as a whole. The project does not ask one tradition to absorb the other. It asks each to correct what the other cannot adequately perceive.

| **Dimension**               | **Democratic tradition**                                                                                             | **Strategic knowledge of 势**                                                                                               |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Primary concern**         | The political significance of individuals; rights, participation, representation and accountability.                 | The operative propensity of an arrangement: how position, relation, timing and condition make outcomes more or less likely. |
| **Characteristic strength** | Supplies a normative reason that each person must count and must be able to appear politically.                      | Perceives the system as an active whole rather than only a sequence of separate decisions.                                  |
| **Characteristic limit**    | May recognise people only after institutions have rendered them visible as voters, claimants, cases or complainants. | Traditionally seats the reader as ruler or commander and treats people as elements to be positioned.                        |
| **Needed transformation**   | Gain configurational perception of the conditions governing whether individuals can appear and matter.               | Reverse orientation: enable people to recognise, contest and alter how they are being arranged.                             |

## 4. What democracy contributes

Democracy supplies the normative direction of the transformation. It asserts that individuals are not merely resources, functional components or objects of administration. Their experiences, differences and capacities to act possess political significance. Strategic knowledge must therefore become answerable to the people whose conduct and circumstances it helps arrange.

However, the democratic commitment to individuals may be applied too late. Institutions often recognise people only after they have become legible in approved forms: voter, citizen, claimant, complainant, case, data subject or rights-holder. The arrangement governing whether a person can enter one of these forms may remain outside democratic attention.

A person can formally possess rights while lacking the practical conditions required to appear as someone capable of exercising them. A complaint may be fragmented into isolated cases; evidence may be held by the institution being challenged; review may occur after irreversible consequences; or the categories available to the person may misdescribe the harm. In such cases, democracy retains its stated commitment to individuals while losing the operational conditions that make the commitment real.

## 5. What 势 contributes

势 is used here as a wider Chinese configurational resource rather than as a doctrine belonging to a single text. It directs attention to the operative propensity of an arrangement. This propensity is not simply a property residing inside an actor or object, nor is it equivalent to a list of sequential causes. It concerns how positions, relations, timing, conditions and available paths combine so that certain actions become easy, difficult, likely, costly or nearly unavoidable.

This permits an earlier democratic question: before asking whether an individual made a free choice or whether an identifiable decision violated a rule, what was the arrangement already making likely? It directs attention to the environment of actionability rather than only to the action that eventually became visible.

The Art of War provides one strategically explicit articulation of this reasoning, but it is neither treated as the exclusive source of 势 nor made to carry the full genealogy of the concept. Its particular value to this project is its preservation of configurational and negative knowledge: knowledge about concealment, vulnerability, positioning, momentum, misdirection, asymmetry and the production of likely outcomes. Such knowledge is politically dangerous when monopolised by rulers, but politically disabling when ordinary people lack the concepts needed to recognise its operation.

## 6. Democratically transposing negative strategic knowledge

Democratically transposing strategic knowledge changes more than its audience. It preserves configurational perception while changing its direction, subject, purpose and test of success.

**Direction.** From arranging people toward enabling people to recognise how they are being arranged.

**Subject.** From the commander who controls a field toward the participants whose lives and options constitute that field.

**Purpose.** From producing advantage over others toward making configurational power publicly perceptible and contestable.

**Test of success.** From whether the strategy achieves the commander’s objective toward whether affected people gain meaningful perceptual and corrective capacity.

Negative knowledge is not made democratic by pretending that deception, coercion or manipulation do not exist. It becomes democratic when knowledge of these operations can be used defensively and diagnostically by those exposed to them. Concealment becomes knowledge of how concealment is produced. Vulnerability becomes knowledge of how vulnerabilities are located and exploited. Momentum becomes knowledge of how an arrangement makes reversal progressively harder. Positioning becomes knowledge of how people acquire unequal capacities to define, decide and correct.

> **TRANSPOSITION**  
> The project preserves knowledge about how arrangements acquire operative propensity while changing its political orientation, enabling people to recognise and contest how they are being arranged.

## 7. Reconstructing the organism

To describe a democratic system as a living organism is not necessarily to subordinate individuals to a superior whole. That conclusion depends upon a limited conception of organism: a bounded entity whose health has priority over replaceable parts and whose needs can be interpreted by a central authority. This is already an administrative image of an organism, not a neutral account of living organisation.

The organism proposed here is a living arrangement whose continuation depends upon the appearance, differentiation, responsiveness and interaction of what composes it. The whole does not first exist and then assign functions to passive parts. It continually arises through relations among differentiated participants. No single observing position exhausts what the organism is.

Under this understanding, the political appearance of individuals is not merely a protection granted against the whole. It is one of the means through which the whole perceives and corrects itself. Complaints, disagreements, refusals and local experiences may carry information that the dominant representation of the system cannot produce internally.

When a system suppresses individuals in order to preserve a declared image of institutional health, it may preserve administrative continuity while damaging its living capacities. The system still operates, but loses channels through which it could encounter its own effects. The relevant sacrifice is therefore not an individual made for the health of an organism. It is the organism’s perceptual and corrective life sacrificed for the preservation of a closed representation of the whole.

**Individual appearance.** The practical capacity of a person’s experience, difference or refusal to become perceptible as politically consequential and capable of entering collective correction.

**Living democratic organism.** An operatively integrated arrangement that sustains itself through distributed perception, differentiation and correction rather than through the suppression of its participants into a single authorised representation.

## 8. Democratic configurational intelligence

Democratic configurational intelligence is the capacity of a democratic arrangement to perceive how its own configuration conditions the appearance, action and corrective standing of individuals. It joins two commitments that are often separated: individuals must count, and the whole arrangement determining how they can count must itself be perceptible.

It is not equivalent to collecting more opinions. Nor does it require the fiction that every perspective can be fully represented or reconciled. Its concern is whether materially affected positions can disclose consequences, challenge the categories that describe them and acquire enough jurisdiction to change the arrangement.

### 8.1 Configurational self-composition

A configuration is not necessarily organised as a whole by a person or central authority. Rules, technologies, classifications, institutional purposes, incentives, timings, available data, burdens, workarounds and responses can encounter one another and become mutually conditioning. Participants seek usable connections among these elements in order to complete local tasks. Those connections may accumulate into an operative order even when nobody perceives, intends or controls that order as a configuration.

To say that a configuration has a life of its own is therefore to describe configurational self-composition, not an independent organism detached from human action. The arrangement continues through people, artefacts and institutions, but it can acquire persistence, direction and selective responsiveness beyond any participant's plan. Each local adaptation may make sense from its own position while adding momentum to a pattern that becomes increasingly difficult to see or reverse.

Jurisdictional recession is one possible result. It need not be deliberately manufactured. Ways of organising evidence, authority, time, review and responsibility may compose a configuration in which effects remain visible locally but cannot return with enough force to revise what produces them. The system has not merely failed to receive feedback; it has progressively organised the conditions under which feedback loses standing, meaning or destination.

This is why configuration requires active stewardship rather than an assumption that it will correct itself. Stewardship means observing how relations and momentum are composing the field, identifying which local adaptations are becoming system-forming, and restoring alteration routes before the pattern hardens. It must also account for agency: deliberate actors can exploit vulnerabilities, redirect existing momentum or ride an already favourable configuration without having created the whole arrangement.

**Configurational self-composition.** The process through which connected elements and local adaptations become mutually conditioning and produce an operative order that no participant needs to have designed or understood as a whole.

> **STEWARDSHIP IMPLICATION**  
> Do not ask only who designed the arrangement. Ask what relations are composing it, what momentum they are accumulating, which corrections they are making less available, and who can exploit or redirect the resulting propensity.

### 8.2 Five diagnostic questions

**Seat.** Who is presumed to use the knowledge, and from what operational position?

**Capacity.** What power, visibility, time, resources and control does the knowledge assume its user possesses?

**Appearance.** Which people and consequences can become visible within the arrangement, and in what authorised forms?

**Revisability.** Which positions may change the categories, models and objects through which the situation is known?

**Corrective force.** Can knowledge from affected positions interrupt or redirect the arrangement, or can it only be recorded after the outcome?

## 9. Positional renewal and the limits of feedback

Democratic configurational intelligence cannot treat situated knowledge as a permanent credential. A person’s knowledge is produced through a particular position, time and set of relations. If that person later acquires institutional recognition, resources or authority, the configuration from which they observe has changed. Their earlier account may remain historically valid, but it does not automatically represent people who now occupy the position they once held.

Recognition also changes the observer. A researcher, advocate or designer whose account becomes institutionally trusted begins to participate in the authority-producing arrangement they describe. The framework must therefore map not only government, affected people and formal decision-makers, but also the researcher, the analytic tool, its adopters and the authority that adoption gives them. Reflexivity is not a declaration of personal humility; it is an examination of how a change in position changes what can be seen, heard and altered.

### 9.1 Continuing revision rather than permanent representation

No person is required to represent a position permanently. Democratic configurational intelligence instead requires continuing routes through which knowledge from current occupants can enter, contradict established accounts and revise the shared map. Fresh knowledge does not erase earlier knowledge: it discloses changes in the field, differences among occupants and consequences that a recognised observer may no longer encounter. The authority of the map must remain downstream of this capacity for revision.

> **POSITIONAL-RENEWAL PRINCIPLE**  
> No situated account should acquire permanent representative authority merely because its holder later gains institutional recognition. The map must preserve routes through which current and differently positioned participants can revise both the account and the authority attached to its author.

### 9.2 Feedback as a conditional intervention

Feedback is not inherently democratic or corrective. It may be coercively extracted, selectively manufactured, stripped of context, routed to an actor without alteration power, used to legitimate a settled decision, or made costly and dangerous for the person providing it. A system can visibly collect feedback while preserving the configuration that prevents it from mattering.

The design question is therefore not how to maximise feedback. It is whether knowledge can enter safely, retain its meaning, reach a position with relevant alteration capacity and remain capable of revising the object, category or direction at issue. Where these conditions are absent, the appropriate response may be to protect anonymity, verify strategic claims, relocate the receiving point, defer collection until protection exists, constrain how information may be used, or refuse an extractive feedback process altogether.

> **CONDITIONAL-FEEDBACK PRINCIPLE**  
> Configure the conditions under which knowledge may enter, travel and acquire corrective force; do not assume that more feedback, participation or visibility is automatically beneficial.

### 9.3 Operational requirements

**Position and time.** Record the position, dependencies and time from which an account was produced, rather than converting lived knowledge into a permanent identity claim.

**Observer effects.** Map how the researcher, tool and adopting institution change the distribution of visibility, credibility and alteration power.

**Revision routes.** Maintain practical means for current and differently positioned participants to amend the map and challenge its authorised interpretation.

**Feedback conditions.** Assess safety, meaning retention, destination, alteration power and reversibility before treating information return as corrective.

**Authority audit.** Re-examine who gains authority when the framework is adopted, which knowledge becomes easier to hear and which becomes easier to exclude.

### 9.4 Relationship to cybernetics

Cybernetics offers broad languages of observation, feedback, reflexivity and system regulation. Democratic configurational intelligence does not take those propositions as a sufficient foundation. It asks the prior political and configurational questions that a general instruction to observe oneself or incorporate feedback leaves unresolved: who may observe, whose account becomes authoritative, who bears the burden and risk of becoming visible, where alteration power resides, and under what conditions feedback should be admitted, protected, verified, deferred, relocated or refused.

The relation is therefore interrogative rather than derivative. Democratic configurational intelligence can contribute to cybernetic inquiry by specifying when recursive observation is democratically useful and when it reproduces the configuration it claims to correct.

## 10. Relationship to jurisdictional recession

Jurisdictional recession occurs when the positions that experience consequences, carry burdens or detect errors lose effective standing over the processes producing them. Their knowledge may exist, but it cannot travel far enough to alter the operative object or decision. Meanwhile, technical, administrative or formal representations acquire expanding authority.

Democratic configurational intelligence addresses the perceptual side of this recession. It asks where the arrangement has separated the production of effects from the capacity to recognise and correct them. Positional democratisation addresses the knowledge side: it reconstructs the tools of perception so that those carrying consequences are not forced to translate their experience entirely into the categories of the authority that produced the problem.

The relationship can be stated compactly: jurisdictional recession is the primary diagnosis; democratic configurational intelligence identifies the capacity needed to perceive the configuration producing that recession; democratic transposition is the emerging constructive direction; and positional democratisation tests whether affected positions can genuinely perceive, contest and revise the arrangement. The latter concepts remain developing implications rather than conclusions assumed in advance.

### 10.1 Configurational reading across positions

Democratic transposition follows how perception and alteration capacity change across connected positions. Knowledge of coordination, leverage and system-wide action is mapped together with distributed burden, visibility, options and routes of return. Differences among positions become evidence about the configuration: where information travels, where it changes meaning, which responses acquire force, and which positions can revise the shared representation and subsequent direction.

### 10.2 Western field and configurational reading

The intended use is not a balanced comparison between complete Chinese and Western traditions. Contemporary legal, administrative, computational and accountability systems form the operative field, largely through Western-derived institutional arrangements. Chinese configurational resources supply an additional sensing and reconstructive capacity within that field. Affected reality remains active: workaround, delay, overload, refusal, repeated exception, compensatory labour and residual harm are field responses through which an arrangement may reveal what its authorised representation cannot see.

Western ruler-oriented knowledge also requires transposition. Statecraft, raison d’état, military strategy, colonial administration, scientific management and technocratic expertise often presume a sovereign, managerial or expert seat. Democratic transposition neither rejects these bodies of knowledge nor merely distributes them. It asks how their capacities for coordination and system perception change when those governed by the arrangement can revise its objects, purposes and corrective routes.

### 10.3 Field-response principle

A system does not encounter reality only through approved measurements. Reality continues to adjust around the arrangement. The resulting friction may appear as noncompliance, exception, workaround or inefficiency, yet may carry information about invalid categories, withdrawn return paths or displaced load. Democratic configurational intelligence requires a route by which such responses can challenge production logic before they are normalised, individualised or used to reinforce it.

## 11. Illustrative application: Robodebt

Robodebt can be approached not only as a chain of unlawful or defective decisions but as an arrangement that governed the appearance of affected individuals. People encountered debts generated through an institutional and computational process, while their experiences of error appeared as separate cases requiring individual response. The system’s formal object could travel at scale; the person’s corrective knowledge travelled slowly and conditionally.

From a configurational perspective, the question is not only who authorised a decision. It is how data matching, evidentiary burdens, automated correspondence, review pathways, institutional incentives and temporal asymmetries combined to produce a propensity toward enforcement and against correction. The arrangement could continue even while many of the people inside it carried information that its central representation was wrong.

From the reconstructed-organism perspective, these people were not external disturbances to an otherwise functioning system. Their experiences were part of the system’s distributed capacity to perceive what it had become. The failure occurred because this perception lacked sufficient jurisdiction to revise the operative arrangement.

## 12. Boundaries and neighbouring traditions

This framework is adjacent to standpoint epistemology, situated knowledge, participatory action research, epistemic injustice, democratisation of expertise and design justice. These traditions establish that knowledge is socially positioned, that marginalised positions can disclose relations obscured from dominant positions, and that affected communities should participate in producing knowledge and design.

The proposed contribution is narrower and more operational. It focuses on actionable knowledge’s implied seat and on the configurational conditions through which affected knowledge either acquires or loses corrective force. Participation matters, but participation alone does not guarantee that the internal architecture of the knowledge has changed. A process can include affected people while continuing to require them to speak as administrators, accept the institution’s objects and adopt the temporal or evidentiary capacities of a privileged user.

The framework also does not claim that every perspective is equally accurate or that all conflicts should dissolve into consensus. It asks whether the arrangement permits consequences to become perceptible from the positions where they occur, and whether those perceptions can genuinely revise collective action.

## 13. Provisional theses

**1.** Knowledge can be publicly accessible while remaining positionally closed.

**2.** Every body of actionable knowledge contains an implied operational seat.

**3.** Democratically transposing configurational knowledge requires changing its political orientation, not merely widening its audience.

**4.** Democracy cannot protect individuals if it cannot perceive the arrangements governing whether they can appear politically.

**5.** The configurational perception offered by 势 must be transformed from its traditional alignment with command.

**6.** A living democratic organism depends upon distributed appearance and correction; suppressing these capacities preserves only a closed representation of the whole.

**7.** Jurisdictional recession occurs when knowledge of consequences remains present but loses sufficient standing to revise the processes producing them.

**8.** Democratic configurational intelligence is therefore both an epistemic capacity and a design requirement for socio-technical governance.

**9.** Field responses are neither self-interpreting truths nor mere noise; their democratic significance depends on whether they can revise the arrangement producing them.

**10.** Situated knowledge remains position- and time-indexed; institutional recognition must not convert an earlier account into permanent representative authority.

**11.** Feedback is a conditional intervention whose democratic value depends on safety, meaning retention, routing, alteration power and revisability.

**12.** Configurations can become operatively ordered through mutually conditioning relations and local adaptations without being designed or perceived as a whole.

**13.** Deliberate actors may exploit, redirect or ride a self-composing configuration without being its sole author; analysis must hold emergence and agency together.

**14.** The circulation of knowledge does not distribute its producer's position; the operative meaning and consequences of uptake must be examined at each receiving position.

**15.** Criticism should attach to the configuration that produces the identified problem rather than being displaced automatically onto downstream readers.

## 14. Working definition

> **WORKING DEFINITION**  
> Democratic configurational intelligence is the distributed, positionally renewable capacity to perceive and revise how an operatively integrated arrangement composes itself and conditions the appearance, action and corrective standing of individuals, including through field responses that its authorised representation may exclude. Democratic transposition reorients configurational knowledge toward people governed or burdened by an arrangement; positional democratisation tests whether its implied seat and practical revisability have actually changed. Feedback counts as corrective only when the conditions of its production, travel, protection and alteration power allow it to change the configuration.
